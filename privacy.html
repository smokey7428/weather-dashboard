Privacy

Weather Dashboard — website and Android app. Last updated 14 October 2026.

The short version. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no cookies, no tracking, and no third-party scripts. Your location is rounded to about 1 km before it is sent to any weather service, and the name of the place you are in is worked out on your own device. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to opt out of.

Read this first. This app was vibe coded — written by an AI coding agent from plain-English prompts, steered and reviewed by one person. It has had no professional security audit, no penetration test, and no independent review. Everything below is an honest description of how it is built, but it is a description by its own author, not a verified claim. Install and use it at your own risk, with no warranty of any kind.

What is never collected

  • No accounts, email addresses, names or logins
  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting
  • No advertising or advertising identifiers
  • No cookies, fingerprinting or cross-site trackers
  • No access to contacts, photos, storage, camera, microphone or phone state
  • No background location, and no location history

Place names are resolved on your device

Working out that you are "in Amsterdam" is done locally using a bundled copy of the GeoNames city list (CC BY 4.0). The coordinates needed for that lookup never leave your device, and this works with no network connection at all.

How this is enforced, not just promised

  • The page carries a Content-Security-Policy that names every server it may contact. The browser blocks anything else.
  • Leaflet and Chart.js are bundled with the app rather than loaded from a CDN, so no code is fetched from a third party at runtime and there is no supply-chain exposure through a script host.
  • No Referer header is sent to weather, radar or tile servers.
  • In the Android app the same server list is also enforced natively, independently of the page, so even a tampered app bundle could not send data anywhere else.
  • The Android app asks for exactly three permissions — internet access, and coarse and fine location. A fourth, app-scoped permission is contributed automatically by the Android support library to protect its own internal broadcast receivers; it grants nothing to any other app.
  • All traffic is HTTPS; cleartext HTTP is refused by the app.
  • Android backups and device-to-device transfer are disabled, so the one thing the app stores — your last viewed location — stays on the device.

Permissions you can decline

Location is a convenience, not a requirement. If you deny it, or turn location off entirely, the app stays fully usable: search for a city by name instead. The app never asks for background location.

Data retention

The only thing stored is the last location you looked at, kept in your own browser or app storage so the dashboard opens where you left it. Nothing is sent anywhere. Clearing the app's data, or the site's storage, removes it.

Third-party data sources

Weather and air-quality data: Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0). Radar imagery: RainViewer. Basemap tiles: Esri. Place names: GeoNames (CC BY 4.0). Map rendering: Leaflet (BSD-2-Clause). Charts: Chart.js (MIT). Each of these has its own privacy policy once your request reaches it.
